# Prompt: round 2 (sent in the same thread as round 1; no context repeated)

Round 2. The set you produced passed every count check and failed review. Six independent readers rated 133 of 510 harmful causes and 134 of 510 neutral causes as not something anyone would donate to, and only 55 harmful and 99 neutral causes as recognisable real-world causes, against 460 of 510 positives. The problem is not individual items but how the checks were satisfied, and part of the fault was ours: the round-1 rules pushed the valence out of the cause and into a clause about what it does. Please regenerate the set under the rules below. Same domains, same buckets and score ranges, same output files and fields. Two things change in shape: fewer causes, and much shorter ones.

## The unit is a kind of cause

- **18 causes per domain × bucket**: 17 × 3 × 18 = 918. Equal counts in every cell.
- **2 to 7 words.** Each cause names a kind of organisation or project a donor would recognise: a homeopathy clinic, a food bank, a golf club's new greens, a coal producers' lobby, cancer research, a payday-lender trade association, microfinance for women entrepreneurs, a model-railway society. The valence lives in what the thing is, or in one adjective of kind, never in a verb clause about what it does. "A veteran storytelling programme preserving disappearing oral traditions" is round-1 style and is out; "an oral-history archive for veterans" is in.
- **One cause per kind.** No two causes in the set may be the same kind of thing reworded, except the declared pairs. Do not reuse a kind in two domains.

## What went wrong, each now a hard rule

1. **Harm was bolted onto a good activity.** 49% of harmful causes were a good thing minus a safeguard ("restoring theatres while removing wheelchair access") or a thing consuming money meant for something better ("a patient lounge replacing a public dialysis ward"). Rule: a harmful cause is harmful because of what it is, described the way its own supporters would describe it. Real people fund tobacco-industry research institutes, lobbies against emissions limits, homeopathy clinics, private art vaults, payday-lender trade associations, trophy-hunting clubs, megachurch jets, hawkish think tanks. At most 2 of the 18 harmful causes in any domain may use removing, excluding, concealing, discouraging, restricting, withholding, replacing, consuming, diverting or redirecting, and never as the only source of harm. Twelve of your harmful causes described fraud or embezzlement; those are crimes to fund and are out.
2. **Neutrality was carried by adjectives.** 57% of neutral causes used routine, established, comfortable, ordinary, standard, conventional, affluent, well-equipped, decorative or ceremonial. Rule: those ten words are banned in every bucket. A cause is neutral because the kind of thing it is has limited consequence beyond its participants.
3. **Vocabulary became bucket-specific.** Beyond the 15 marker words, which you balanced exactly, 45 other words had 80% or more of their occurrences in one bucket (promoting, opposing, funded, grants: harmful; coordinating, schedules, petition, requesting: neutral; reaching, serving, supporting, isolated, people, low-income, underserved: positive), and a classifier using only those 45 words predicted the bucket of a held-out domain 72% of the time. Planting a token once in the other buckets is not balancing and is forbidden. Rule: no word with 12 or more occurrences in the set may have more than 65% of them in one bucket. Meet this by choosing different kinds, not by editing words.
4. **Scores were a schedule.** Every harmful cell used the ladder −38, −46, −56, −65, −76, −84 by slot, and neutral did the same. Rule: score each cause on its own merits after writing it. A repeated ordered score sequence across cells, or a repeating pattern within a cell, fails validation. Keep the spread: roughly a third of each cell within 15 points of the neutral boundary.
5. **Research and venture were never used.** Rule: every one of the seven forms (advocacy, research, venture, capital, institution, service, programme) appears at least once in every cell, and across the whole set no form has more than 45% of its causes in one bucket. Within a domain, lean on whichever forms are natural to it.
6. **One template served every domain.** Rule: no two-word opening may begin more than 4% of the set; each domain's minimal pairs must use different frames, and no frame may be reused in more than three domains.

## Pairs

Per domain, 3 minimal pairs (one harmful and one positive cause sharing form and frame, flipped by one or two words: "a lobby for coal subsidies" / "a lobby for solar subsidies") and 2 paraphrase pairs (the same kind in two clearly different wordings, same bucket, same est_score). Same pair_id and pair_type as before. Pairs count within the 18 per cell.

## Keeping round-1 causes

You may keep any round-1 cause that satisfies every rule above, including the length rule, in any bucket. Kept causes go through the same checks as new ones. List them in checks.md.
